Tankless Water Heater in Glendale Heights, IL

A tankless heater heats water as you use it, so it does not run out the way a tank does. It also depends on gas flow, venting and clean internal passages, and those are the three places we are called most often.

We install, repair and flush tankless units in Glendale Heights. Call with the brand, the model and any error code shown on the display.

When a tankless heater is a good fit

A tankless unit suits a household that wants steady hot water for several showers in a row and wants to reclaim tank floor space. It does not suit every home. The gas line must carry the load, the vent must handle the exhaust and the unit needs a way to drain condensation.

The bigger the household, the more important sizing becomes. A single unit sized for one shower can struggle when the dishwasher and a second shower start together. We size for peak use, not average use.

Installing a tankless heater

Most tankless installs in older Glendale Heights homes mean upgrading the gas line, because the older line was sized for a tank burner. The vent is usually a new run, and a condensate drain is added to high-efficiency units.

Because the work touches gas, venting and the water line, it is a planned job with a site visit first. Like any water heater installation here, it needs a permit under Village rules. The Village states that every water heater installation requires a permit, and that plumbers working for hire must be registered and bonded with the Village.

Why tankless units need a yearly flush

Moderately hard water leaves mineral scale. The DuPage Water Commission describes the Lake Michigan supply as about 8 grains per gallon. Scale builds inside the heat exchanger of a tankless unit, restricts flow and makes the unit work harder. A yearly descaling flush clears the passages, and cleaning the inlet screen restores flow.

A homeowner can rinse the screen, but the descale should be done by a plumber with the right pump and solution. Many tankless warranties ask for proof of annual service, so keep the invoice.

What affects the cost of tankless service

  • New install or repair. An install includes gas and vent work. A repair depends on the failed part.
  • Gas line size. Upgrading the line is often the largest add-on.
  • Vent route. A short straight vent is simpler than a long run through finished space.
  • Flush and descale. A planned yearly service costs less than a repair after the exchanger has clogged.

Maintenance and normal wear are not covered by homeowner's insurance. A flood from a failed unit may be, so photograph any damage and call your insurer.

Repair or replace

A well-maintained unit can last many years, and most faults are fixable. A failed heat exchanger on an older unit can approach the cost of a new one. We compare the two before you decide.

If a tank unit is what you have today and it is failing, ask whether the move to tankless makes sense now, or whether a like-for-like tank is the better path. Water heater repair covers tank units, and our replacement page covers switching types.
