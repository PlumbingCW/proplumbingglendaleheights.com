Emergency Hydro Jetting in Glendale Heights, IL

A main line that keeps backing up is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is open for it. Stop using water, keep the washer and dishwasher off and call so a plumber can look at the line and decide whether jetting is the right tool.

Sewer backup: what to do first

When the lowest drain in the house gurgles or overflows, the blockage is usually in the main line. Stop using every fixture. Keep family and pets out of the affected area, and avoid running a wet vacuum on sewage, which can spread contamination. Then call.

We dispatch a plumber the same day when one is available, day or night, and the plumber runs a camera to see the pipe before choosing a method. Jetting is powerful, but it is not right for every pipe.

What hydro jetting does

A jetter sends water at high pressure through a hose with a nozzle that throws jets backward and forward. It scrubs the pipe wall, cutting grease, scale and soft roots and flushing the debris out through the line. A snake bores a hole through a blockage. A jet cleans the whole pipe wall.

That difference matters when grease has coated a pipe for years. A snake can punch a hole, and the pipe silts up again within weeks. Jetting cleans the diameter back to something close to full.

When jetting is the wrong tool

A pipe that is cracked, collapsed or badly offset can be damaged by high pressure. That is why the camera comes first. If the camera shows a break, the right fix is on the sewer line repair page, and a jet would only push water into the soil.

Older homes in Glendale Heights built from the late 1950s may have clay, cast iron or other legacy drain pipe. These materials can fail at joints and with age. We look before we recommend.

Common causes of main line trouble

Grease in a kitchen line, wipes flushed down toilets and tree roots entering through joints are the three causes we see most. Heavy rain can raise the load on shared sewers. A main that backs up only when it rains is worth a camera look, because the cause may be roots or a belly in the pipe.

A sewer line cleaning visit with a drum machine solves some of these. Jetting solves the ones where buildup and silt are the real problem. A camera inspection sorts the two apart.

What affects the cost of hydro jetting

  • Length and size of line. A longer or larger line takes more time.
  • Access point. A cleanout makes it simple. Without one, a plumber may need to remove a toilet.
  • Camera inspection. A look before and after is advisable and priced clearly.
  • Severity. Years of grease take longer to cut than a recent clog.

Jetting is maintenance, not an insurance claim. Sewage damage inside the house may be covered under a water backup endorsement, which many policies add separately, so check.

Keeping the line clear afterward

After jetting, keep grease out of the kitchen sink and wipes out of toilets. A line that jetted clean can silt again if the same habits continue. Some households with a history of grease or roots schedule a yearly or two-yearly clean.

Neighbors in Wheaton and Carol Stream call us for the same reasons, and many have long runs of older pipe.

Standards and sources for this job

Jetting a sewer line is maintenance work that sits under the Illinois Plumbing Code in Title 77 of the Illinois Administrative Code, and a camera inspection before jetting is how we confirm the pipe can take the pressure. The Village's sump pump brochure also reminds owners that sump water may not enter the sanitary sewer.
