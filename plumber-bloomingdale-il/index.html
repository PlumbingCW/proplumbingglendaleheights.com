What a Bloomingdale plumbing call usually looks like

Most Bloomingdale calls come from homes of mixed age. The village was first incorporated in 1889 and re-incorporated in 1923, and its older streets near Old Town carry homes from several eras. Newer subdivisions sit beside them, so we never assume the pipe type from the address. For an active leak, call and say where the water is.

A plumber is dispatched the same day when one is open, including nights and weekends. If the leak is behind a wall and you cannot find it, leak detection traces it with thermal imaging before anyone cuts drywall.

Around Old Town and the Lake Street corridor

Old Town Bloomingdale sits at Lake Street and Bloomingdale Road, in restored buildings on the village's first settlement site. Older buildings and shared lines are common near there, and a restaurant or shop with a slow floor drain needs a quick answer. We take commercial plumbing calls here at any hour.

Homes in the residential streets around the village trail system tend toward drain clogs and water heater failures more than anything dramatic. If a basement drain backs up after a hard rain, tell the dispatcher, because that is a different job from a single clogged sink.

Water supply and what it does to your plumbing

Bloomingdale is a contract customer of the DuPage Water Commission, which supplies Lake Michigan water bought from the City of Chicago. The Commission describes that water as about 8 grains per gallon, or 137 mg/L as calcium carbonate, which is moderately hard. Scale from hard water builds in tank heaters and faucet aerators.

A tank heater here that has never been flushed often rumbles by its tenth year, and then water heater replacement is the sensible fix. A softener slows the scale on the next one.
