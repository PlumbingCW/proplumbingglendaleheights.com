When a water line break is an emergency

A wet patch in the yard, a sudden loss of pressure or water that sounds like it is running with every fixture off can mean the buried service line has failed. A break flushes soil and can undermine a driveway or a foundation. Close the main, call and describe what you saw.

A line that has frozen in a cold winter is also an emergency, because it can split before the thaw. Keep every faucet closed and do not try to thaw a buried line with a flame. Call us and the dispatcher books the next open plumber.

Who owns what

The service line runs from the water main in the street to the house. The Village or the water provider normally owns and maintains the part up to the meter, and the owner is responsible for the pipe from the meter into the house.

Rules differ by address, so call Public Works at (630) 260-6040 to confirm where yours ends. We find the break first. If it is on the utility side, we tell you and you report it. If it is on your side, we repair it.

Lead lines and testing

Older service lines in some communities contain lead, and any home built decades ago may have one. If you are concerned about lead, the Village directs residents to Public Works at (630) 260-6040 for testing and information. Replacement of a lead line is a project we can help you plan.

The water here is Lake Michigan water supplied through the DuPage Water Commission, and the Village states that it adds no chemicals to it. The service line is a separate question, which is why testing a tap is worth asking about.

What affects the cost of a water line repair or replacement

  • Length and depth. A longer, deeper line costs more to dig.
  • Surface restoration. Driveways, sidewalks and landscaping add to the work.
  • Method. Open trench and trenchless methods are priced differently.
  • Permits and locates. Utility locates and permits are part of the planning.

Service line damage from wear is often not covered by homeowner's insurance, though some policies offer an add-on for service lines. Check your policy before work begins.

Protecting your water line

Know where your main shutoff is and test it each year. Keep large tree roots away from the line, and protect exposed pipes in winter. If your pressure is very high, a pressure reducing valve protects the line and the fixtures behind it.

If the leak turns out to be inside the house instead, burst pipe repair is the quicker path. For an old line with repeated trouble, repiping the interior along with the service line is worth considering. Neighbors in Wheaton ask us the same questions.

Standards and sources for this job

Service line work follows the Illinois Plumbing Code and depends on where the Village's line ends and yours begins. The Village's 2024 water quality report says lead mainly comes from service lines and home plumbing, and it directs testing questions to Public Works at (630) 260-6040.
