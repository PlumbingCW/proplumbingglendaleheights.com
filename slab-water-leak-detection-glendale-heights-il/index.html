Slab Water Leak Detection in Glendale Heights, IL

A slab leak is a water leak in a pipe that runs under or inside a concrete slab floor. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water when everything is off, a damp carpet edge and a water bill that jumps with no other cause. A plumber confirms it with a meter test and locates it with listening gear before any concrete is cut.

This guide explains the signs, the detection steps and the repair options.

By the Glendale Heights Pro Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-16 · Updated 2026-11-16

☎ (630) 822-9290

Do slab leaks happen in Glendale Heights

They can, in any home with supply lines under a slab. Many homes here have basements, which means supply lines run through the basement ceiling and are easy to reach. A house on a slab, a basement slab with lines embedded in it or an addition built on a slab can have lines beneath the floor.

Glendale Heights had 104 residents when it petitioned to incorporate in 1959, and it counted 33,176 in 2020. The housing that filled in between is a mix of ranch homes, split-levels, apartments and condominiums. Which of those has lines under a slab depends on the building, not on the village.

A plumber looks at your house instead of assuming.

A hot water line under a slab often shows as a warm floor, since the leaking water heats the concrete. A cold line is harder to notice and usually shows as a rising bill or a damp spot.

How a plumber finds it

The meter test comes first, to prove water is flowing with every fixture off. Then the plumber isolates sections of piping, shutting valves one at a time to see which section loses pressure. Acoustic listening gear picks up the sound of escaping water through the slab, and a thermal camera can show a warm path.

Once the leak is located to within a small area, the plumber marks it. Only then is the slab opened. Without that care, a floor can be broken in the wrong place and the leak still run.

Repair options

Re-routing is often cheaper than breaking concrete and lasts longer where pipes are failing. A single leak in a sound system may be best repaired in place. We explain each option and the price before work begins.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

  • Close the main shutoff if the floor is wet or the sound of running water continues
  • Avoid standing on a floor that feels hot, because the line beneath may be a hot water line
  • Move rugs, furniture and electronics away from damp areas
  • Photograph the damp spot, the meter and the date before cleanup
  • Do not drill or cut into the slab to look for the pipe

What the Village's own history says about local housing

According to the Village of Glendale Heights' history page, a petition to incorporate was filed on June 16, 1959, when the population was 104, and that the court declared the Village organized on July 13, 1959. It says the community has ranch homes, split-level homes, apartments and condominiums.

Homes from that building era are now more than 60 years old, so supply lines of that age may still be in service in some houses. The Village's 2024 water quality report points residents to Public Works at (630) 260-6040 for testing, and its brochures list Community Development at (630) 260-6030 for permits.

Slab and floor repairs that open structural elements are worth asking the Village about before work.

Why speed matters

A slab leak undermines the soil beneath the slab and soaks flooring and framing. It also raises your bill every day. Closing the main stops the loss but leaves you without water, so a plumber should come soon. If the floor is warm, the water is hot, so avoid standing on a hot spot.

Mold can follow a slow leak under flooring. Call earlier than you think you need to.

Insurance

Policies differ on slab leaks. Some cover the damage the leak causes but not the cost of finding or fixing the pipe. Some cover access and tear-out. Ask your insurer before work begins, and keep photos and receipts.
