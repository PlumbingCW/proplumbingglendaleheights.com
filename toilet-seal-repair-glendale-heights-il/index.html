Toilet Seal Repair in Glendale Heights, IL: What to Know

A toilet seal is the wax ring or rubber gasket between the toilet and the floor drain flange. When it fails, water leaks at the base of the toilet or sewer gas leaks into the room. The repair is to lift the toilet, replace the seal, check the flange and set the toilet down again on a new seal.

This guide explains the signs, what the repair involves and how to choose a plumber who is properly licensed in Illinois.

By the Glendale Heights Pro Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-16 · Updated 2026-11-16

Signs the seal has failed

Water around the base of the toilet after flushing is the main sign. It may be a small damp ring or a puddle. A toilet that rocks, a sewer smell in the bathroom, a soft or stained floor near the base or a ceiling stain in the room below can all point to a failed seal.

Do not assume the leak comes from the seal. A cracked tank, a loose tank bolt and a failing supply line can all leave water near the base. Dry the area, flush, and watch where the first drop appears. If it appears at the floor joint, the seal is the likely cause.

Why seals fail

Common causes and how they are fixed
CauseWhat happensFix
AgeThe wax hardens and cracksNew wax ring
Loose or rocking toiletThe seal is broken by movementReset with new seal and secure bolts
Damaged flangeThe ring cannot seatRepair or replace the flange
Floor settling or raised floorGap grows after new flooringUse an extender ring or a thicker seal
Repeated clogs and plungingStrains the sealClear the clog and reseat
A toilet should never rock. Even a slight movement breaks the seal again. The repair is only as good as the flange and the floor beneath it.

What the repair involves

The plumber shuts off the water, drains the tank and bowl and disconnects the supply line. The toilet is lifted off the flange and set aside. The old wax is scraped away, and the flange is checked for cracks, corrosion and height.

A new seal goes on, the toilet is set down squarely and the bolts are tightened evenly. The toilet is then reconnected, tested with several flushes and checked for leaks at the base and at the supply.

If the subfloor is soft, the plumber tells you before closing up, because rotted wood must be repaired for the toilet to sit properly.

Can you do it yourself

A confident homeowner can replace a wax ring, but it takes a heavy lift and careful setting. A porcelain toilet is awkward and easy to crack. Lining up the bolts and holding the toilet level as it seats is a two-person job in many bathrooms.

Call a plumber if the flange is broken, if the floor is soft, if you cannot lift the toilet safely or if the toilet is on an upper floor where a leak could reach a ceiling below.

Hiring a licensed plumber in Illinois

According to the Illinois Department of Public Health, plumbers are licensed under the Illinois Plumbing License Law. IDPH states that any licensed plumber who performs work for another person is a plumbing contractor. Plumbing contractors must register with the state, pay an annual fee and carry general liability, bodily injury, property damage and workers' compensation insurance.

IDPH hosts a plumbing license verification tool that links from its plumbing page, so you can check a plumber before work starts. For work inside Glendale Heights, the Village's published water heater brochure says contracted installations require a licensed plumber who is registered and bonded with the Village.

What to do when a toilet leaks at the base

  • Turn the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise and stop flushing
  • Mop up the water and put a towel around the base so you can see whether it returns
  • Do not use the toilet until the seal is replaced, because each flush adds water to the floor
  • Photograph any stain on the ceiling below for your insurer
  • Call a plumber if the floor feels soft or spongy near the base

What a fair repair visit looks like

A good plumber explains the problem before starting. They show you the old seal and the condition of the flange. They tell you if the floor needs repair and give you the price before work begins. They also check the toilet for other leaks, such as the fill valve or the supply line.

If you are told the whole toilet must be replaced because of a seal leak, ask why. A leaking seal does not damage the toilet. A cracked bowl or tank does.

Related fixes at the same visit

Since the toilet is already off the floor, it is a good time to address other items. Replace an old supply line. Replace a worn flapper or fill valve. If the flange is corroded, repair it now. Our toilet repair and installation team handles those items together.

If the toilet clogs often, the cause may be in the line and not the seal, and drain cleaning can tell you.

Protecting the floor

A leak at the toilet base soaks the subfloor over time. If the floor feels soft, probe it with a screwdriver. Wood that is damp or spongy needs replacing before a new seal will work for long. If the leak has gone on for months, ask about mold behind the baseboard.
