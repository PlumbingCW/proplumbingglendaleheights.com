Emergency Faucet Repair in Glendale Heights, IL

A faucet that will not shut off or is spraying water under the sink is urgent, and our 24/7 line is open. Close the stop valve under the sink by turning it clockwise, and if there is none, close the main, then call so a plumber can be scheduled the same day.

When a faucet problem cannot wait

A steady drip is a nuisance and a waste, not an emergency. A faucet that cannot be turned off, a spray from the base or handle and water collecting under the sink cabinet are different. Those can damage the cabinet and floor in a short time.

The first move is the small oval or round valve on each supply line under the sink, called the stop valve. Turn it clockwise. If it will not turn or leaks, close the main and call. An old stop valve that has seized is a repair in itself.

The right fix depends on the faucet design, and cartridge, ceramic disc and compression faucets all fail differently. We identify the type and choose the right part.

Why scale shows up in faucets here

The DuPage Water Commission describes the water it supplies as about 8 grains per gallon, which is moderately hard. Mineral scale builds up in aerators and in cartridges, making flow weak and handles stiff over time. Cleaning an aerator is a quick fix, and soaking it in vinegar often restores flow.

If your faucets and showerheads scale up quickly, water softening reduces the problem at the source. A faucet in hard water may need a cartridge every few years, even when it is a good model.

Repair or replace the faucet

An older faucet with a worn cartridge is usually worth repairing if parts are available. A faucet with corroded body, pitted finish or discontinued parts is better replaced. A replacement also lets you upgrade to a pull-down spray or a better finish. We install the faucet you choose, or we can suggest one that fits your sink.

Check how many holes your sink has before buying, because it decides which faucets fit. The plumber also checks the supply lines and the stop valves, and replaces them if they are weak.

What affects the cost of faucet repair

  • Repair or replacement. Cartridges and seals cost less than a new faucet.
  • Faucet type. Kitchen pull-downs and widespread bath faucets take more time.
  • Stop valves. Seized valves may need replacing during the visit.
  • Access. A cramped vanity or a stone countertop adds work.

Homeowner's insurance does not cover a worn faucet, but it may cover a sudden leak that damages a cabinet or floor. Take photos before cleaning up.
