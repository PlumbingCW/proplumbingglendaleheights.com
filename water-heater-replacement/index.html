Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Glendale Heights, IL

A water heater that is leaking from the tank cannot be repaired, and our 24/7 line is open for exactly that call. Close the cold water valve above the heater, switch off the power or gas, and call so a replacement can be scheduled, often the same day.

While you wait, put a bucket under the leak, keep children away from hot water and do not drain the tank over carpet.

When a failed tank becomes an emergency

A tank that is dripping, bulging or standing in water has reached the end. Left alone, a small drip can become a full tank release into a finished basement. The tank holds 40 to 50 gallons in a typical home, and all of it can leave at once.

If the tank is intact but the water is only lukewarm, a repair may still help, and water heater repair covers that. We tell you which situation you are in when you call.

What the replacement visit includes

A plumber confirms the failed tank, drains it and removes it. The new heater is set and connected, the water supply and gas or electric are hooked up, and the relief valve and discharge are installed to current practice. The vent is checked on a gas unit. Then the heater is filled, purged and tested.

We also check for an expansion tank where a closed system makes it advisable. The old tank is hauled away. You get a clear price before the old unit comes out, and any change gets discussed first.

Permits and local rules

The Village of Glendale Heights states that every water heater installation requires a permit, whether the job is done by a plumber or a homeowner. It also says plumbers working for hire must be registered and bonded with the Village. A plumber who skips the permit leaves you with an unpermitted installation.

Illinois separately licenses plumbers through the Illinois Department of Public Health. Check any plumber against the state's license tool before you hire. The Village's Community Development office is at (630) 260-6030 if you want to confirm the permit steps yourself.

What affects the cost of water heater replacement

  • Tank size and type. A 40-gallon standard tank is different from a high-efficiency or hybrid unit.
  • Venting and gas work. Older vents or undersized gas lines can add work.
  • Code upgrades. Expansion tanks, drain pans and discharge piping may be required.
  • Haul-away and permit. These are part of the job, and we state them in the price.

Insurance usually does not cover replacement of an old heater that wore out. It may cover water damage from a sudden tank failure, so photograph everything and call your insurer.

How long a tank lasts here

Tank life depends on water chemistry and maintenance. Moderately hard water, which the DuPage Water Commission puts near 8 grains per gallon, leaves scale that shortens life, and a tank never flushed can fail sooner. A heater past ten years with rust at the base or seeping fittings is a strong candidate for replacement.

Putting a softener on the supply is one way to protect the next tank, and water softener and filtration explains what it does and does not solve. Neighbors in Wheaton ask us the same question.

Standards and sources for this job

The Village's water heater brochure says a permit is required for every installation. It also describes a relief valve discharge pipe of three-quarter inch metallic pipe that ends no more than 6 inches above the floor, and a drain pan with indirect waste for heaters above the basement. We confirm current requirements before we set a tank.
