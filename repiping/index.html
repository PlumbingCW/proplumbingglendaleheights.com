Repiping in Glendale Heights, IL

Repiping replaces the water supply lines in a house when they have worn out, leaked repeatedly or lost pressure. It is a planned project and it is easier to schedule before the next failure than after it.

If a pipe is bursting now, call the 24/7 line and ask for burst pipe repair. Plan the repipe once the water is under control.

☎ (630) 822-9290

Signs it is time to repipe

One leak is a repair. Repeated leaks in different spots, discolored water, low pressure on every fixture and visible corrosion at joints suggest the pipe is failing as a whole. Pipes of the same age tend to fail in the same way.

Homes in Glendale Heights built from the late 1950s onward may still carry original supply lines, depending on what has been done since. We do not assume a pipe type from the house age. We look at what is actually there, and a leak detection visit is a good first step if you cannot tell.

A whole-house repipe replaces every supply line from the main to each fixture. A partial repipe does a section. We tell you honestly if a partial repair is enough, because it saves you money, and we tell you when a partial will only delay the same job.

Materials

PEX tubing and copper are the two common choices. PEX is flexible, goes in with fewer joints and resists freezing damage better. Copper is durable, familiar and long lived. The right choice depends on the house, the water and your preference. We explain both and install the one you choose.

Moderately hard water, which the DuPage Water Commission describes as about 8 grains per gallon, can scale inside any pipe over time. A softener protects new lines and fixtures. Either material can last decades with proper installation.

What the work looks like

A repipe means access. Plumbers open small sections of drywall, run new lines and patch the openings. In homes with unfinished basements, most of the run is visible and easy to reach, so the job is faster and less messy. A finished basement and plaster walls add work. Planning shortens the disruption.

We walk the house, mark the access points and agree on the order of work. The water is off for part of the day, and we tell you when. Drywall repair and painting are usually not included in the plumbing price, so we flag them up front.

What affects the cost of repiping

  • House size and layout. More fixtures and floors mean more pipe and time.
  • Material. PEX and copper differ in price and labor.
  • Access. Open framing is simpler than finished walls.
  • Finish work. Drywall, paint and flooring repairs are usually a separate cost.

A planned repipe is not an insurance claim. If a leak caused sudden damage, the damage may be covered even if the pipe is not, so document it and ask your insurer.

Permits and licensed work

Repiping is permitted work in most places, and the Village of Glendale Heights requires plumbers working for hire to be registered and bonded with the Village. Illinois licenses plumbers through the Illinois Department of Public Health. Ask any plumber for proof, and check state licenses with the IDPH verification tool.

If your home is old enough to have a lead service line, ask the Village's Public Works at (630) 260-6040 about testing and options. That is a separate question from interior repiping, and it is worth asking about in the same call.

After the repipe

Pressure usually improves, water runs clear and the system gets a fresh start. We test the new lines under pressure before closing the walls, and we show you the shutoffs. For fixtures you plan to add, plumbing installation fits into the same schedule. Addison and other neighbors schedule the same way.

Standards and sources for this job

Repiping follows the Illinois Plumbing Code in Title 77 of the Illinois Administrative Code and normally needs a permit. The Village's brochures say contracted plumbers must be registered and bonded with the Village, and its water quality report notes that lead mainly comes from service lines and home plumbing.
