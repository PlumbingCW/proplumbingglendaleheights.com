Damaged Sewer Line Repair: Glendale Heights, IL Homeowner Guide

A damaged sewer line shows up as repeated backups, slow drains on every floor, gurgling toilets, sewage smells and soggy ground in the yard. A camera inspection confirms it and locates the break. The repair then depends on what the camera shows: a spot repair for a short break, lining for a pipe that is cracked but intact or replacement for a collapsed section.

This guide explains the signs, the options and the decisions that save money.

By the Glendale Heights Pro Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-16 · Updated 2026-11-16

Signs the line is damaged

One slow drain is rarely a sewer problem. Several symptoms together are. Look for slow drains in more than one fixture, a toilet that bubbles when you run a sink, a floor drain that backs up and a wet or sunken spot on the lawn over the line. A sewage smell outdoors can mean a break.

Roots are a frequent cause. They enter through small gaps at joints, grow into dense masses and trap waste. A line that clears for a few months and then clogs again is a candidate for a camera look instead of another cleaning.

What a camera shows

A waterproof camera on a flexible cable runs through the line and records what it sees. It shows roots, cracks, offset joints, bellies where water pools, collapsed sections and heavy buildup. The plumber marks the location above ground, so you know where any damage sits.

Repair options

Spot repair digs to the damage and replaces a short section. It suits a single break and costs less than a full replacement. Lining inserts a resin-coated liner that hardens inside the old pipe and forms a new pipe within a pipe. It works on pipe that is cracked but not collapsed and needs little digging.

Pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through the old line while breaking the old one apart. It suits a long failure where lining will not work. Open-cut replacement digs the full length and is used when other methods do not fit. Each has a place. We explain which fits your footage.

What affects the price

Length of the damaged section, depth, soil conditions and the surface above all matter. A line under a lawn costs less to restore than one under a driveway or a patio. Permits and utility locates add steps. Access points matter too, because a cleanout simplifies the work.

A written price should name the method, the length, the surface restoration and the permit. Be careful with a quote that does not mention the camera findings.

Glendale Heights facts for sewer repair planning

The Village's sump pump brochure states that no sump pump may be connected to discharge into a sanitary sewer, citing Section 7-5-2 of the Village code. A sump discharge tied into the sanitary line adds stormwater to a pipe sized for household waste, and it is worth checking when a line backs up after heavy rain.

For the water and sewer utility side, the Village's 2024 water quality report lists its Utilities Division and Public Works at (630) 260-6040, which is a sensible first call to learn where the Village's responsibility ends and yours begins at your address.

For permits on repair work, Community Development at (630) 260-6030 is the contact the Village's brochures give.

Before you dig or line

Do not agree to digging without seeing the footage. Ask for a copy of the video. If the plan is lining, ask what condition the pipe must be in and whether your pipe qualifies. If the plan is replacement, ask why lining will not work. A reasonable plumber answers these without pressure.

Get a second opinion on any large repair. The footage can be shown to another plumber, and a second look costs little compared with the repair.

Insurance

Aging pipe is often excluded from a homeowner's policy. Sewage backup damage inside the house may be covered through an endorsement, which is often a separate add-on. Some policies offer service line coverage. Read your policy and ask your insurer before work begins.

Prevent a repeat

Keep wipes and paper towels out of toilets. Keep grease out of the kitchen drain. Keep large trees from growing over the line where possible. A sewer inspection every few years on an older line gives you a baseline, and hydro jetting keeps buildup in check where roots are not the problem.
