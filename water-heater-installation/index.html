Water Heater Installation in Glendale Heights, IL

A planned water heater installation is the calm version of a water heater emergency. You choose the type and size, the permit is pulled and the work is scheduled for a time that suits the household.

This page covers new installs and fuel or type changes. If your tank is already leaking, use water heater replacement and call the 24/7 line.

☎ (630) 822-9290

Start with the right size

A heater sized too small runs out in the middle of a shower. One sized too large wastes fuel keeping extra water hot. Size depends on the number of people at home, how many bathrooms run at once and whether you have a soaking tub or a large kitchen load.

We ask about those habits before we recommend a size. We also look at the space, because a unit that fits a utility closet in one home may not fit the next. Tell us the make and model of the heater you have now, and we can often match it quickly.

The permit and who is allowed to do the work

The Village of Glendale Heights says every water heater installation requires a permit, whether a plumber or the homeowner does it. It also states that plumbers working for hire must be registered and bonded with the Village. Both points matter, because an unpermitted install can surface when you sell the home or file an insurance claim.

Illinois licenses plumbers through the Illinois Department of Public Health, and its license verification tool lets you check any plumber. For permit questions, the Village's Community Development office can be reached at (630) 260-6030.

What happens on install day

A plumber shuts off the water and drains the old tank if there is one. The new unit is set on a stable base, with a drain pan where the location calls for one. Supply lines, the temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe, and the vent or electrical connection are completed.

Then the system is filled, purged of air and tested. We walk through the controls with you. We tell you where the shutoffs are and when the first flush should happen. The old tank is hauled away unless you ask otherwise.

What affects the cost of a water heater installation

  • Heater type and size. Tankless and hybrid units cost more than standard tanks.
  • Gas, vent and electrical work. New vent or circuit work is priced separately and stated up front.
  • Access and layout. A basement with a straight run is quicker than a tight crawlspace.
  • Permit and haul-away. Both are covered in the quote, so there is no surprise at the end.

A planned install is normally not an insurance claim. If the old tank failed and flooded the basement, ask your insurer about the water damage and keep the photos.

Water quality and the new heater

The DuPage Water Commission describes the Lake Michigan water it supplies as about 8 grains per gallon, which is moderately hard. Scale builds inside a heater over years, so a new tank benefits from a yearly flush. A softener on the incoming line cuts the scale load for the heater and for fixtures.

If you plan to renovate a bathroom or finish a basement soon, plan the heater at the same time. Moving the unit once, with the new layout drawn up, costs less than doing it twice. Our plumbing installation team handles the rest of that work.
