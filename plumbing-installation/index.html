Plumbing Installation in Glendale Heights, IL

Plumbing installation covers the planned work that adds, moves or upgrades fixtures. That includes new sinks, toilets, tubs and showers, laundry hookups, basement bathrooms and outdoor spigots.

If something is leaking right now, call the 24/7 line for repair first, and plan the installation when the water is under control.

Basement bathrooms and finishing

Adding a basement bathroom is a common project in Glendale Heights, where many homes have basements. The drain height decides whether it can run by gravity or needs an ejector pump. An ejector adds equipment but makes a bathroom possible below the sewer line. Plan the plumbing before the framing is closed.

A rough-in inspection is normally required, and the Village can confirm the process through Community Development at (630) 260-6030. Moving a drain after the walls are up costs far more than planning it first.

Permits and licensed work

Plumbing alterations normally need a permit. The Village of Glendale Heights states that plumbers working for hire must be registered and bonded with the Village. Illinois licenses plumbers through the Illinois Department of Public Health, and the state's tool lets you check any license. We tell you when a permit applies and what inspection follows.

Skipping the permit can create trouble at resale or in an insurance claim, and it can leave unsafe work behind a wall.

Water-side details worth planning

New fixtures add demand. We check supply pipe size and pressure for any large addition, such as a second shower or a soaking tub. High pressure is hard on fixtures and appliances, and a pressure reducing valve is a modest addition when the reading is high.

Moderately hard water, which the DuPage Water Commission describes as about 8 grains per gallon, affects faucets and showerheads. If you are renovating, ask us about softening or filtration while the walls are open. A new heater for the added load is a common part of a larger project, and water heater installation covers that.

What affects the cost of a plumbing installation

  • Scope. A single fixture is small. A bathroom is a project.
  • Access. Open framing and a basement ceiling are simpler than finished walls.
  • Fixtures. Their price is separate from labor, and we state both.
  • Permits and inspections. These are part of planning and we list them in the price.

Planned improvements are not an insurance claim. Keep receipts and permit records, because they support the home's value and any later claim.

Planning a renovation

If you are remodeling a kitchen or bath, tell us early. The best time to fix old supply lines or drains is when the walls are open. If the lines are failing, a partial repipe done during the renovation is far cheaper than opening the wall again.

For appliance hookups, such as a dishwasher, ice maker or washing machine, we install valves, drains and braided lines. A garbage disposal often goes in at the same time as a new sink. Bloomingdale homeowners schedule the same combined visits.

What to do before any installation visit

  • Find and test your main shutoff so the plumber can isolate the work
  • Clear a path to the work area and move stored items away from walls
  • Do not cut into walls or floors to look for pipes
  • Ask the plumber whether a permit applies and who pulls it
  • Turn off power to any circuit near the work if wiring will be exposed

Standards and sources for this job

New plumbing is built to the Illinois Plumbing Code and normally needs a permit and inspection. The Village's brochures direct residents to Community Development at (630) 260-6030, and say contractors must be licensed and bonded with the Village before permits are issued.
