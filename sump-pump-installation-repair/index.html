Sump pump failure: what to do first

Check the simple things in two minutes. Make sure the pump is plugged in and the outlet works. Reset the breaker or the GFCI. Lift the float arm by hand and listen for the motor. If the pump runs but the pit does not drop, the discharge line or the check valve may be blocked.

If the pump will not run, the pit is filling and the basement is at risk, call us. We dispatch the same day when a plumber is open, day or night. Never stand in water to touch electrical equipment. Switch the breaker off first from dry ground, or wait for the plumber.

Pumps have a limited life. A pump past about ten years, or one that cycles all day, is overdue. Replacing a worn pump before the rain is cheaper than replacing it in the middle of a flood.

Village rules on discharge

The Village of Glendale Heights publishes a sump pump brochure. It states that a sump pump may not discharge into the sanitary sewer, and that it may not discharge onto a neighbor's lot or a public street.

The discharge should go to the yard in a way that drains away from the house and does not create a nuisance. We follow those rules when we install or repair a pump. If your pump discharges into a floor drain or the sanitary line, we flag it, because that can overload the sewer and cause backups.

Community Development at (630) 260-6030 can answer permit and discharge questions for your lot.

Choosing a pump and backup

A submersible pump is the common choice. It sits in the pit and is quiet. A pedestal pump has its motor above the pit and is easier to service. We size the pump to the pit and the inflow, and we use a quality check valve to keep water from running back.

A battery backup pump is worth considering because storms often cut power. A backup keeps water moving for several hours. A water-powered backup is another option in some homes, though it uses municipal water. We explain the options and you choose.

What affects the cost of sump pump work

  • Repair or replace. A float or check valve is small. A new pump is more.
  • Pump type and power. Higher capacity and backup systems cost more.
  • Pit and discharge work. A damaged pit or a rerouted line adds work.
  • Electrical. A dedicated GFCI outlet may be needed.

Sump pump failure damage is often excluded unless you carry water backup coverage, which is usually an add-on to a homeowner's policy. Ask your insurer before the next storm.

Keeping the pump ready

Test your pump in spring by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. It should start, empty the pit and shut off. Clean the intake and check the discharge line outside for ice and debris in winter. Keep the discharge from being blocked. A basement that floods repeatedly may need more than a pump.

A perimeter drain system, grading changes and gutter extensions address the water at the source, and basement waterproofing covers those options. If the water in the pit tastes or smells of sewage, call us, because sewer line cleaning or repair may be involved.

Local notes

Heavy spring rains and quick snowmelt send water to basements across DuPage County. Bloomingdale and Glendale Heights are close neighbors, and many homes on both sides of the border rely on a pump.
