Plumber in Glendale Lakes, Glendale Heights, IL

Glendale Lakes is the part of Glendale Heights built around the village's golf course, east of Schmale Road and south of Fullerton Avenue. Residents who want plumbing help can phone in and explain the issue. This page covers what sets plumbing in a townhome and house mix apart from a standalone home.

Real estate records describe a blend of townhomes and single-family houses built from about 1980 into the late 1990s. We use that only as a general guide. Nothing here claims to know what is inside your walls.

Where Glendale Lakes sits

The village owns and manages Glendale Lakes Golf Club, which its facilities page lists at 1550 President Street. A golf directory describes the 18-hole public course as a Dick Nugent design that opened in 1987. That course gives the community its name and its best-known landmark.

Sales listings in the area name Golfview Drive, Club Drive and President Street. One real estate directory describes a townhome group inside the community, the Greens of Glendale Lakes, as built between 1988 and 1990. We found no official boundary map, so treat Schmale Road and Fullerton Avenue as approximate anchors.

Across Fullerton Avenue to the north, Pheasant Ridge and Glendale Terrace are a separate single-family area.

Townhomes share more than a wall

In an attached home, a leak in one unit can reach the unit below or beside it. Water heaters often sit in a closet or utility space with little room to work. Some supply and drain lines may be an association matter rather than the owner's, and the rules differ from one association to the next.

Real estate listings show association fees for homes in this area, so your documents are the place to start. Ask who maintains which lines before you spend money, and ask whether the association wants notice before work begins.

Before you call about a leak in an attached home

  • Find the shutoff for your unit and make sure it turns.
  • Look at the ceiling or wall of the neighboring space for stains.
  • Photograph the damage before anything is moved.
  • Read your association rules on who repairs which pipes.
  • Note when it started and which fixtures were in use.

Water heaters, scale and the village permit

Check the permit rule before a tank is swapped. The village's water heater information says new installations are permitted no matter who does the labor, and Community Development at (630) 260-6030 can walk you through the process. A repair that keeps the same tank usually does not need one.

The village buys Lake Michigan water through the DuPage Water Commission, which describes it as about 8 grains per gallon, or 137 mg/L as calcium carbonate. That is moderately hard, so scale can build up in a tank over the years. If your heater is old or leaking, read the options on the replacement and repair pages.
