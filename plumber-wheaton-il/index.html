Three things Wheaton homeowners ask us most

First, what is behind a high water bill. Second, whether a heater is worth repairing. Third, how to deal with a basement that backs up. Each has a straightforward answer once a plumber has looked.

A high bill is usually a leak

Check the meter with every fixture off. If it moves, the leak is in your lines. Wheaton's own water report notes that lead mostly comes from service lines and home plumbing, and it suggests running a tap for 30 seconds to 2 minutes after water has sat for hours.

For hidden leaks, leak detection finds the source and the water service line page covers buried pipe.

Repair or replace a heater

Wheaton expanded rapidly from the 1950s, so many homes have heaters well past their first decade. If the tank leaks, replace it. If a part failed on a younger tank, repair may be worth it. Repair and replacement prices are both given.

Backups

A basement drain that overflows is a main line problem more often than a fixture problem. We scope the line and tell you what we find.

Where Wheaton's water comes from

Wheaton's 2023 water report says its supply comes from Lake Michigan through the City of Chicago, and its six emergency wells were not used for finished water that year. The DuPage Water Commission also lists the City of Wheaton as a contract customer and describes its Lake Michigan water as about 8 grains per gallon.

That level of hardness is moderate. It leaves scale in heaters and fixtures. For water questions specific to your account, the city's water office handles billing and quality, and it lists a separate line for after-hours emergencies.

Housing and geography

Wheaton has about 20,900 housing units. Rapid growth from the 1950s means many homes are 50 to 70 years old, with some much older near downtown and Wheaton College. The old county courthouse and the Grand Theater, listed on the National Register of Historic Places, mark the downtown core.

Main roads include Roosevelt Road, Naperville Road, Geneva Road, Butterfield Road and North Avenue. Winfield and Warrenville sit to the west and southwest, and we reach both from our Wheaton dispatch. Call and ask.
