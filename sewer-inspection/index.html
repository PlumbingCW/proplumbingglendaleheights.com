Sewer Inspection in Glendale Heights, IL

A sewer inspection puts a waterproof camera inside the line so you can see what is there. It is the right step before buying an older home, after a repeat backup or when you want a baseline on a line you suspect.

This is a planned service. If sewage is backing up right now, call our 24/7 line and ask for sewer line repair.

☎ (630) 822-9290

When a camera inspection makes sense

Buying a home built in the late 1950s or later is the most common reason. A general home inspection does not look inside the sewer lateral, and a collapsed or rooted line can be a large expense after closing. A camera look takes the guesswork out of the purchase price.

Other reasons include repeat clogs, a drain that gurgles, wet patches over the line, slow drains after heavy rain and a line that was cleaned and clogged again within months. A camera shows the cause, so you spend on the right fix.

Inspecting before you buy

Ask the seller for permission, schedule the inspection during your option period and bring your agent into the conversation. If the camera shows a failing line, you can negotiate the price, ask for a credit or walk away. Bring the recording to your agent and an insurer if needed. A hydro-jetted line looks different from a neglected one.

If the line is clogged and cannot be viewed, the plumber may need to clear it first, and sewer line cleaning is the step that gets the camera through.

Why Glendale Heights lines deserve a look

Most Glendale Heights homes date from the late 1950s onward, and lateral pipe from that period can be clay or cast iron, depending on the house. These materials can crack, separate and admit roots. The material in your own line is a fact the camera confirms. Do not assume either way.

The Village of Glendale Heights Public Works office, at (630) 260-6040, can answer questions about the public sewer connection. If you find a problem at the connection, ask them who is responsible for which section.

What affects the cost of a sewer inspection

  • Access. A cleanout makes the job quick. No cleanout means a longer setup.
  • Line length. A long lateral takes longer to scope.
  • Cleaning first. A line that must be cleared before the camera can pass adds a step.
  • Report. A recorded video and a written summary are included when you request them.

Inspection fees are not covered by homeowner's insurance, but the findings may help you decide on repairs and on a claim if damage follows.

After the inspection

If the line is sound, you have a baseline for the next inspection in a few years. If it is not, you have footage to compare quotes and a clear picture of the work. Our plumbers explain each repair option and the price, and you can take your time.

Standards and sources for this job

A camera inspection records the condition of the lateral and does not alter it. If the footage points to repair, the work follows the Illinois Plumbing Code under Title 77 of the Illinois Administrative Code and any Village permit that applies.
