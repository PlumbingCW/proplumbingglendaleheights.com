Bathroom Faucet Repair: Glendale Heights, IL Homeowner Guide

Most bathroom faucet problems come from three parts: the cartridge or washer inside the handle, the aerator at the tip of the spout and the O-rings that seal the base. A drip from the spout points to the cartridge or seat. Low flow points to the aerator. A wet base points to the O-rings.

This guide shows how to tell them apart, what you can do yourself and when to call a plumber, with notes on the moderately hard water that runs through Glendale Heights taps.

Match the symptom to the part

Start by watching exactly where the water appears. A faucet that drips from the spout after you shut it off has a worn cartridge, washer or valve seat. A faucet that leaks around the handle has worn seals. A faucet that sprays sideways or runs weakly usually has scale in the aerator.

What a homeowner can safely try

Close the small stop valves under the sink before touching anything. Unscrew the aerator from the spout and soak it in vinegar for an hour. Rinse and replace it. That fixes more weak-flow complaints than any other step. If the faucet drips, you can replace the cartridge, but you must match the exact model.

Take the old cartridge to a supply store, or photograph the faucet and note the brand. A wrong cartridge leaks again or will not fit. If the stop valves will not close fully, stop. Closing the main and calling a plumber is better than forcing a valve that might break.

Why scale shows up here

Glendale Heights water comes through the DuPage Water Commission, which describes the Lake Michigan supply as about 8 grains per gallon. That is moderately hard water. Scale builds in aerators and on cartridge surfaces, and it makes handles stiff over time.

You will see white crust at the tip of the spout, then a thinner stream, then a handle that grinds. Cleaning the aerator every few months slows this down. A water softener reduces it at the source. In a house with several bathrooms, softening can pay for itself in fewer fixture repairs.

Glendale Heights water facts for faucet owners

The Village buys its drinking water from the DuPage Water Commission, which receives Lake Michigan water from the City of Chicago. The Village's 2024 water quality report says it does not add chemicals to the water. The Commission's own water FAQ gives the hardness as about 8 grains per gallon, or 137 mg/L as calcium carbonate.

For questions about your own tap, the Village's report directs residents to Public Works at (630) 260-6040. The report also notes that lead in drinking water mainly comes from service lines and home plumbing, and it advises flushing taps for several minutes after water has sat unused.

Repair or replace the faucet

Repair makes sense when the faucet body is sound and parts are available. A faucet with a corroded body, a pitted finish or a discontinued cartridge is a replacement candidate. So is one that has leaked more than once in a year. When you buy a new faucet, check the hole count in your sink or vanity top.

A single-hole, three-hole and widespread faucet are not interchangeable. Choose a faucet with a solid brass body, and ask whether a replacement cartridge will be sold for it in five years.

When to call a plumber

Call a plumber when a stop valve will not close, when water is already under the sink, when the supply lines are old braided hose that has kinked or when the faucet is part of a larger bathroom update. A leak at a supply connection can damage a vanity floor quickly.

Our faucet repair team handles cartridges, stop valves, supply lines and full replacements. If the sink also drains slowly, drain cleaning fits the same visit.

Check the whole bathroom while you are at it

A faucet is the easiest fixture to ignore until it fails. Test the stop valves under the sink by closing and reopening them. Look at the supply lines for rust or bulges. Check the toilet for a running flapper. Small problems found together cost less than the same problems found one at a time during separate visits.

How to avoid the next leak

Wipe the faucet dry after use if the water leaves spots. Clean the aerator twice a year. Do not crank handles hard against the stop. Replace old supply lines when you replace the faucet. These habits add years to a faucet in moderately hard water.
