Sump Pump Repair Cost for Glendale Heights, IL Homeowners

A sump pump repair costs less than a replacement, and the price depends on which part failed. A stuck float or a failed check valve is a small job. A dead motor on a pump near the end of its life is usually a replacement. The discharge line, the pit and any backup add to the price when they need work.

We do not quote without seeing the pump, but this guide shows what each failure usually means and how to decide between repair and replacement.

By the Glendale Heights Pro Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-16 · Updated 2026-11-16

Common failures and what they mean for price

The float switch tells the pump when to run. If it sticks, the pump either never starts or never stops. The check valve keeps water from flowing back into the pit after the pump shuts off. If it fails, the pump cycles constantly.

The motor is the heart of the pump, and a failed motor almost always means replacement.

Stuck floatFree or replace the float switchLow

Age decides repair or replace

A pump past about ten years is on borrowed time. If it needs a repair and is older than that, replacement is often the wiser spend. A younger pump with a failed float or valve is worth repairing. Cycling frequency tells you something too.

A pump that runs every few minutes in dry weather has a failed check valve or a stuck float. A pump that runs constantly in a storm may be undersized. Ask the plumber to compare the pump's capacity to the inflow in your pit.

Backup pumps change the math

A battery backup pump adds cost but protects against the exact moment you need it most, when a storm knocks out power. Many pump failures happen during storms, because storms are when pumps run hardest. A backup also gives the main pump a rest in a flood. Ask about a water alarm too.

A simple alarm near the pit tells you the water is rising before it reaches the floor, and it costs little.

Glendale Heights sump pump rules that shape the price

The Village's sump pump brochure sets the discharge rules, and they affect what a repair can include. It cites Section 7-5-2 of the Village code, which says sump pumps must be installed so storm water does not drain onto neighboring property or onto a street or public way.

It states that no sump pump may be installed and connected so as to discharge into a sanitary sewer.

If your discharge currently goes to a floor drain or the sanitary line, a repair will not be the end of it, because the discharge must be corrected. The brochure also says a permit is required before installing a new sump pit or draintile, and that contractors must be licensed and bonded with the Village.

For specifics, call Community Development at (630) 260-6030.

What to check yourself first

Make sure the pump is plugged in and the outlet works. Reset the GFCI or breaker. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and see whether the float lifts and the pump starts. Look at the outside end of the discharge pipe and clear any ice, leaves or debris.

If the pump runs but the water level does not drop, the check valve may be installed backward or clogged, or the discharge line may be blocked. If the pump will not run at all, do not reach into the pit with the power on.

Why the cheapest fix is not always cheapest

Replacing a float on a ten-year-old pump may buy a month. Replacing the pump may buy ten years. The second costs more today and less over time. We give both numbers and say which we would choose in your place.

A repair done during a storm costs the same as one done in dry weather, but the damage from waiting does not. If water is rising, call the 24/7 line.

Insurance and cost

Sump pump failure damage is often excluded unless you carry water backup coverage. That coverage is usually an add-on. Check your policy before the next storm, not after it. The cost of repairing or replacing the pump itself is normally not covered.

Nearby towns with the same issue

A seasonal routine that saves money

Test the pump each spring and fall. Clean the pit and the intake. Check the discharge outlet before winter. Replace a backup battery every few years. A routine takes ten minutes and often prevents a bad night.
