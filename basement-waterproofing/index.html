Basement Waterproofing in Glendale Heights, IL

Basement waterproofing stops water from entering or moves it out before it can pool. It starts with finding where the water comes from and ends with a drain, a pump and a path for the water to leave.

If water is coming in right now, see sump pump repair and call the 24/7 line. This page covers the planned fix.

Why basements get wet

Water reaches a basement in a few ways. Rain soaks the ground against the wall, and pressure forces water through cracks and the joint where the floor meets the wall. Poor grading sends surface water toward the house. Gutters that dump at the foundation add to it.

A high water table after a long wet spell raises pressure under the floor. Look for the pattern. Water at one corner points to grading or a downspout. A line of seepage along the base of the wall points to the floor-wall joint. A wet floor after every heavy rain points to groundwater and an overloaded pump.

We start with the cheapest steps that address the cause. Many wet basements improve with gutters and grading. Where groundwater is the cause, an interior drain and a sump pump is the usual answer, and we install the whole system.

Interior drain systems

An interior drain runs along the inside of the footing, below the slab edge. It catches water before it reaches the floor and carries it to a sump pit. A pump then moves it out. Done right, it keeps the floor dry even in storms, and it can be paired with a battery backup.

The discharge must follow Village rules. The Village states that a sump pump may not discharge into the sanitary sewer, onto a neighbor's lot or onto a public street. We plan the discharge so water leaves the property legally and drains away from the foundation.

What waterproofing cannot do

Be careful with promises. A sealant painted on a wall does not stop groundwater pressure. A crack injection will not fix a basement with a high water table. We tell you what each method can and cannot do, and we do not guarantee a dry basement without a drainage solution. Moisture also comes from inside.

Condensation on cold pipes, a dryer vent that exhausts into the basement and an unvented bathroom raise humidity. A dehumidifier helps with those, though it does not replace drainage. If you see mold, test and clean it before finishing the space.

What affects the cost of basement waterproofing

  • Cause of the water. Gutters and grading cost far less than a full drain system.
  • Length of drain. Perimeter length decides most of the labor.
  • Sump pump and backup. Pump type and battery backup add to the price.
  • Finish work. Removing and replacing floor edges or wall finishes is separate.

Water damage from a sudden event may be covered by homeowner's insurance, but seepage and groundwater usually are not. Check whether you carry water backup coverage.

Before you finish a basement

Do not finish a basement that gets wet. Drywall and carpet will absorb water and breed mold. Fix the water first, then finish. If you plan a bathroom, plan the drains and the sump together, and see plumbing installation for planning. If your sump pump is old, replacing it before the next rainy season is the cheapest insurance.

A failing pump and a drain system together make a weak setup. Neighbors in Carol Stream ask the same question before finishing their basements.

Local ground conditions

Soils in the Chicago area are often heavy clay in places, and clay holds water against foundations. We do not guess at your lot. We look at the grade, the downspouts and the walls, and we recommend what the site needs.

Standards and sources for this job

The Village's sump pump brochure, citing Sections 7-5-2 and 9-4-5 of the Village code, sets the rules on where a sump pump may discharge. It says footing drains near a storm sewer must connect to the storm system, and that a permit is required before installing a new sump pit or draintile.
