Water Softener & Filtration in Glendale Heights, IL

Water in Glendale Heights is moderately hard, which leaves scale on fixtures, in water heaters and on glass. A softener or a filter can help, and the right choice depends on what bothers you and what your water actually contains.

We install and service both. Call, and we start with what the supply really delivers, not with a sales pitch.

☎ (630) 822-9290

What the water here contains

The Village of Glendale Heights buys Lake Michigan water from the DuPage Water Commission, which gets it from the City of Chicago. The Village says it adds no chemicals to that water. The Commission describes the supply as about 8 grains per gallon, or about 137 mg/L as calcium carbonate, which falls in the moderately hard range.

That hardness is a mineral content issue, not a safety issue. It leaves scale in water heaters, on faucet aerators, on showerheads and as spots on glass. The Village directs residents with questions about their own tap to Public Works at (630) 260-6040.

A softener protects pipes, heaters and fixtures from scale, and it makes soap lather more easily. A filter targets taste, odor or particles. Many households need one or the other, not both. We explain each so you buy what solves your problem.

Is a softener worth it here

At about 8 grains per gallon, a softener is a comfort and maintenance choice, not a necessity. A tank water heater lasts longer when scale is controlled, and tankless units benefit most, since scale narrows their heat exchangers. If you have a tankless unit, see tankless water heater for why the annual flush matters either way.

There are costs. A salt softener needs salt, produces brine discharge and has rules about where the brine goes. Some households prefer not to add sodium to drinking water, and a bypass tap for cooking and drinking is a common answer. We help you decide.

Install and service

A softener goes on the main line after the meter and before the water heater, so every hot and cold tap gets treated water. We install a bypass valve, a drain connection for regeneration and a hose bib for the garden if you want hard water outside.

The unit needs a power outlet and a floor drain or standpipe nearby. After installation, we set the hardness and the regeneration schedule using the local supply as a guide. Check the salt level monthly and clean the brine tank once a year. Annual service is a good habit.

What affects the cost of a softener or filter

  • Type and capacity. Larger households need larger units.
  • Installation work. Supply, drain and electrical connections vary by house.
  • Water test. A test of your tap tells us whether a filter is needed.
  • Ongoing costs. Salt and filter changes are regular expenses.

Water treatment equipment is a home improvement, not an insurance matter. Keep the receipts and the service records.

Honest limits

A softener does not make water safe or treat lead. It does not remove bacteria. If you are worried about lead from an older service line or interior plumbing, ask Public Works at (630) 260-6040 about testing, and consider water line replacement if the line is lead.

If scale has already damaged a water heater, water heater replacement is the right fix, followed by softening to protect the new unit. Wheaton and other neighbors ask us about the same decision.

What to do before you choose water treatment equipment

  • Test your tap so you know what you are treating before you buy
  • Ask Public Works at (630) 260-6040 about lead testing if you have concerns
  • Keep a bypass for any tap used for cooking or drinking if sodium matters to you
  • Ask your physician before using softened water as your main drinking source
  • Plan the drain and power outlet before the unit is delivered
