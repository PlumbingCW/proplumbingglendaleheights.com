Older village, older plumbing

Glen Ellyn was incorporated in the 1890s, so its older blocks hold homes that predate the suburban boom by decades. Many have been renovated more than once, with copper, steel and plastic pipe mixed together in the same house. A plumber here reads what is actually in the wall. Owners of older homes often weigh repair against replacement.

We give both prices. Where an old supply line has failed twice, we say so, and repiping a run is worth considering.

Winter and frozen lines

Glen Ellyn winters stress exterior-wall pipes, garage lines and crawlspace runs. A frozen pipe that has not yet split should be left alone with the water off to that line, and the dispatcher can talk you through it. If it has already burst, burst pipe repair is the call.

Do not use an open flame to thaw a pipe. A hair dryer on low heat is safer than anything with a flame, and we recommend calling before you try.

Calls that come from the downtown and Roosevelt Road areas

Shops and restaurants around Main Street and Crescent Boulevard near the Metra station run longer hours than their neighbors, and an after-hours leak is a business emergency. The College of DuPage has its main campus in Glen Ellyn, and rentals and small buildings around it have heaters and drains like any other.

Roosevelt Road carries traffic to I-355, so we can reach the east side of the village quickly when the roads are clear. We do not promise arrival windows, because the route changes with traffic.

Water and sewers

Glen Ellyn is a contract customer of the DuPage Water Commission, which supplies Lake Michigan water described as about 8 grains per gallon. Older houses often have long drain runs, and roots and aging joints are common problems. A sewer camera inspection shows what you have, and is a smart step before buying an older home.

If a backup is mainly grease or silt, cleaning may be enough. If the pipe has failed, sewer line repair with lining or replacement is the lasting fix.

Close neighbors we also serve
