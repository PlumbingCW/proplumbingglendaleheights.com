The first ten minutes of a plumbing emergency

Do these four things while you wait for the plumber

Shut off the waterTurn the main shutoff valve clockwise. In most Glendale Heights homes it sits where the water line enters the basement or utility room, near the meter.
Stop using drainsIf sewage is backing up, do not run the washer, shower or dishwasher. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere.
Kill power near waterIf water is near outlets, appliances or a sump pump, switch off that circuit at the panel only if you can reach it while standing on dry floor.
Call and describe itTell the dispatcher where the water is, how fast it is moving and whether you have already closed the main valve. That tells us what to bring.

Four problems behind most emergency plumber calls here

The first is the burst or leaking supply line. Water that reaches a finished basement ceiling or a wall cavity does damage quickly, so burst pipe repair always starts with the shutoff and a fast repair of the failed section.

If you cannot find where the water is coming from, leak detection uses thermal imaging and acoustic gear to locate it without opening every wall. The second is the backed-up drain or sewer. A single slow tub is usually a local clog that drain cleaning clears the same day.

When several fixtures gurgle or the lowest drain in the house backs up, the blockage is in the main line, and sewer line repair or hydro jetting may be the right answer after a camera look. The third is the water heater that leaks or stops making hot water.

A heater that is dripping from the tank is finished, and water heater replacement is usually the fast fix. If it only lost heat, water heater repair often solves it for far less. The village requires a permit for every water heater installation, which we cover below. The fourth is the sump pump that fails during heavy rain.

Glendale Heights has rules about where a pump may discharge, and a failed pump in a finished basement is a sump pump emergency at any hour.

What the village and Illinois require before a plumber touches your pipes

Illinois licenses plumbers through the Illinois Department of Public Health under the Illinois Plumbing License Law, and a plumbing contractor must also register with the state and carry insurance. IDPH posts a license verification tool, so you can check any plumber before you hire one.

Inside the village, the Department of Community Development publishes a rule that every water heater installation needs a permit, whether a plumber or the homeowner does the job. It also says plumbers working for hire must be registered and bonded with the Village.

A plumber who offers to skip the permit is telling you something about how the rest of the job will go. The village's sump pump brochure adds another rule that matters in heavy rain. A sump pump may not discharge into the sanitary sewer, and it may not send water onto a neighbor's lot or a public street.

We keep both rules in mind on every basement waterproofing and sump job.

Glendale Heights water in numbers

The village buys its water from the DuPage Water Commission, which gets Lake Michigan water from the City of Chicago. The village says it adds no chemicals to that water. The Commission describes its water as about 8 grains per gallon, or 137 mg/L as calcium carbonate, which is moderately hard.

That is enough to leave scale inside a tank water heater over the years, which is why a flush and a look at the anode rod matter, and why some households choose a water softener. To test your own tap or ask about lead, the village directs residents to Public Works at (630) 260-6040.
