Frozen Pipe Repair for Glendale Heights, IL Homes

If a faucet runs only a trickle or nothing in freezing weather, a pipe is probably frozen. Open that faucet, find the cold section of pipe and warm it gently with a hair dryer, never a flame. If you see water, a bulge or frost with a split, shut the main valve and call a plumber, because the pipe has burst or is about to.

This guide explains how to find the freeze, thaw it safely and avoid a flood when the ice melts.

By the Glendale Heights Pro Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-16 · Updated 2026-11-16

Signs of a frozen pipe

A frozen pipe shows up as no water or a weak flow from one or more fixtures during a cold snap. You may see frost on an exposed pipe, hear gurgling or smell an odor if the freeze is in a drain. Pipes along exterior walls, in unheated garages, crawlspaces and attics are the usual victims.

Check which fixtures are affected. If only the kitchen sink is dry, the freeze is likely along that supply line. If the whole house has no water, the freeze may be at the service line or the meter area.

Thaw it safely

Do and do not

Open the faucet so water can flow as it thawsUse a torch, propane heater or open flame
Warm the pipe with a hair dryer on lowUse a heat gun held close to one spot
Wrap the pipe in warm towelsLeave heat sources unattended
Work from the faucet toward the frozen sectionStart thawing in the middle of the pipe
Keep a bucket and towels readyIgnore a split and keep heating
Stop and shut the main if you see a splitTurn the water back on before you check
Heat the pipe slowly. Steam trapped between two ice plugs can burst a pipe, and an open flame can ignite framing. If you cannot reach the pipe or it is inside a wall, do not cut into the wall. Call a plumber.

When it has already burst

A frozen pipe may split without leaking until it thaws. When the ice melts, water pours from the split. Look for wet drywall, dripping ceilings, a hissing sound or a pressure drop after the thaw begins. If you find water, close the main shutoff immediately, open a low faucet to drain the lines and call.

Our burst pipe repair team answers the 24/7 line for this call, and the dispatcher can tell you what to do while the plumber is on the way.

Prevent a freeze

Insulate exposed pipes in garages, crawlspaces and attics. Seal gaps where cold air enters near pipes. Disconnect and drain garden hoses and close the shutoff to outdoor spigots. During a hard freeze, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls and let a faucet drip slowly. Keep the house heated above freezing even if you travel.

If a line freezes every winter, rerouting or insulating it is cheaper than repeated repairs. A plumber can tell you whether the problem is the location of the pipe or a gap in the wall.

Who to call in Glendale Heights when the freeze is on the water service

The Village of Glendale Heights buys its water from the DuPage Water Commission, and the Village's 2024 water quality report lists the Utilities Division and Public Works at (630) 260-6040 as the contact for water questions.

If the whole house is without water and the problem seems to be at the meter or the buried line, that number is the place to learn where the Village's responsibility ends and yours begins at your address.

For repair work that needs a permit, the Village's brochures list Community Development at (630) 260-6030. The Village's water heater brochure says plumbers working under contract must be registered and bonded with the Village, which is worth confirming before you hire anyone during a cold emergency.

Where pipes freeze first

Exterior-wall kitchens and bathrooms, pipes in an unheated attic or garage, outdoor spigots and lines in crawlspaces are the most common. Homes with additions often have pipes in new exterior walls with less insulation than the original house. A plumber who sees your layout can name the weak points. Look at where pipes enter the house, too.

A gap around a pipe through a foundation wall lets cold air hit the line directly.

Insurance

Burst pipe damage is often covered when it is sudden and accidental, but the policy may not cover damage from neglect, such as turning off the heat in a vacant house. Photograph the damage, keep receipts and call your insurer. The pipe repair itself may not be covered.

What a repair looks like

A plumber cuts out the split section, replaces it with new pipe and fittings and tests the system under pressure. If the freeze affected several sections, they may replace more. After repair, the plumber checks other exposed lines and suggests insulation.

If several pipes failed in the same cold snap, ask about repiping the weak run or rerouting it away from the cold wall.
