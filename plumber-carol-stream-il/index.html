A village built from farmland in the late 1950s

Carol Stream grew from farmland that developer Jay Stream began buying in 1957. The first residents moved in on November 1, 1958, at 141 Mohawk, and the village was founded in 1959, so many of its homes are now around 60 years old or more.

That age matters, because water heaters, drain lines and supply lines of that era are reaching the point where failures cluster. We do not guess which pipe is in your wall. We look.

But a home from that period is a reason to ask about the age of the heater and the condition of the main drain before trouble starts.

Sewer and drain trouble comes first

If your lowest drain gurgles or backs up, stop running water and call. In older neighborhoods, main line clogs from roots and grease are common. We run a camera first and then choose the right fix, from sewer line cleaning to hydro jetting when buildup is the problem. A backup after heavy rain deserves a second look.

Check whether your sump pump is discharging into the sanitary sewer, which is a known cause of overload. Rules differ by village, so ask the Carol Stream Public Works office for local requirements. Glendale Heights, for comparison, states that a sump pump may not discharge into the sanitary sewer.

Water and heaters

The Village of Carol Stream is listed among the DuPage Water Commission's contract customers, and the Commission describes its Lake Michigan supply as about 8 grains per gallon. Scale and sediment shorten tank life.

A heater past ten years that rumbles, leaks at the base or runs out quickly is a candidate for replacement, and we give you the repair price too.
