Plumber in Reskins, Glendale Heights, IL

Reskins is one of the oldest residential parts of Glendale Heights and carries the name of the family that began building here in 1958. A published local history says Charles and Harold Reskin, working as Midland Enterprises, bought two farms on Glen Ellyn Road north of North Avenue. The first houses went up on Glen Ellyn Road and Larry Lane near Fullerton Avenue.

Anyone in a house from that era who wants a plumber can phone and explain what is wrong. The sections below separate what we can verify about the area from what only a plumber can learn by looking.

A neighborhood named for a family

The name is still in daily use. The village lists Reskin Park at 8.5 acres, with a ball field, a fitness station and three shelters. A real estate directory lists Charles G. Reskin Elementary among the schools serving the area.

A real estate directory describes Reskins as single-family houses built between 1959 and 1976, east of Glen Ellyn Road and south of Armitage Avenue. Sales in the area appear on Easy Street, Leslie Lane and Lincoln Avenue. We found no official boundary map, so use Reskin Park on East Fullerton Avenue as your anchor.

What the age of a house does and does not tell you

A house from the 1960s may have had its water heater, toilets and faucets replaced several times. Age alone does not tell you what pipe is in the wall, and we will not guess. A plumber should look at your lines before anyone assumes a problem exists. The practical step is to gather what you know.

Ask the previous owner or a neighbor about earlier repairs, and read the date on the water heater label. If a drain keeps slowing down, a camera run through the line shows its real condition, and the sewer inspection page explains what that involves.

Whose pipe is it

On a detached home you generally own the pipe from the house to the point where it meets the village line. Where that point is varies, so call the village. Public Works handles water and sewer from 1615 Glen Ellyn Road, and Community Development at (630) 260-6030 handles permits. Both are in Glendale Heights.

Questions to settle before a bigger plumbing job

  • Which fixtures or lines have been replaced before, and when?
  • Where is the main shutoff, and does it close fully?
  • Has the main drain ever been inspected by camera?
  • Is the age of the water heater printed on its label?
  • Does the job need a village permit?

Toilets, fixtures and heaters

Older fixtures are not automatically bad ones. A running toilet is often a worn flapper or fill valve, and the toilet repair and installation page lists when a swap makes more sense than a repair.

If a tank heater is leaking at the base, replacement is usually the answer, and the village states that a new installation needs a permit. Hard water matters here as it does across the village.

The DuPage Water Commission puts its Lake Michigan supply at about 8 grains per gallon, which can leave scale on heating elements and fixtures. See water heater replacement and drain cleaning for the methods and what drives the price.
