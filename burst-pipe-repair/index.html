When a burst pipe is an emergency

Almost every burst pipe is an emergency, because the water does not stop until the supply is closed. A pinhole spray from a copper elbow can still wet a finished ceiling in an hour. A split in a line feeding an upstairs bathroom can drop through two floors before anyone notices.

The main shutoff is the first tool you own. In most Glendale Heights homes it sits where the water line enters the basement, close to the meter. Turn the handle clockwise until it stops. If the valve will not move, call us at once and say so, because a stuck main valve changes what the plumber brings.

Call at any hour. Nights, weekends and holidays are normal hours for burst pipes, so the line is answered around the clock and the dispatcher books the next open plumber.

What actually causes pipes to fail in homes like these

Glendale Heights grew mostly from the late 1950s onward, so many homes carry supply lines installed decades ago. Water here is moderately hard. The DuPage Water Commission describes its supply as about 8 grains per gallon, and scale and age together can leave older fittings brittle at the joints. The other big cause is winter.

A line along an exterior wall, in a crawlspace or in an unheated garage can freeze, swell and split. The split often shows only after the thaw, when pressure returns and the water starts moving.

A frozen line that has not yet burst should be thawed gently and checked, and our plumber can advise on that over the phone. Past repairs matter too. A pinhole in one section of old copper often means the neighboring sections are thinning.

Finding the leak you cannot see

Some bursts are hidden. A stain on a ceiling, a damp baseboard or a meter that keeps turning with every tap closed points to a leak inside a wall or under the slab. In that case leak detection finds the source with thermal imaging and acoustic gear, which keeps the opened area small and the repair cost down.

If the failed pipe is outside the house, between the street and the meter, the repair belongs on the water service line page instead. We handle both, and the first call sorts it out.

What affects the cost of burst pipe repair

  • Access. A pipe under an open basement ceiling is quick. One behind finished drywall or tile adds time and finish work.
  • Pipe material and size. Copper, PEX and older steel each use different fittings and methods.
  • Scope. One section is a small job. A whole run is a larger one, and we explain the difference before starting.
  • Hour of the call. After-hours work is dispatched the same way, and the price is still agreed before the work begins.

Water damage to floors, walls and belongings may fall under your homeowner's policy, while the pipe itself usually does not. Ask your insurer before you decide, and keep photos and receipts.

After the repair

Once the pipe is fixed, the water-damaged area still needs attention. Wet drywall and insulation should be dried quickly, because damp materials left in a wall invite mold. Ask your insurer whether a restoration company should be involved, and keep every receipt. We can also check the rest of the system.

High pressure shortens the life of pipes and fittings, so a quick pressure reading is worth taking. If the house is a candidate for new fixtures or valves, a repair visit is a good time to plan them. Bloomingdale and nearby neighbors call us for the same reason.

Standards and sources for this job

Pipe repair in Illinois follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, which the Illinois Department of Public Health lists under Title 77 of the Illinois Administrative Code. The work must be done by a plumber licensed under the Illinois Plumbing License Law, 225 ILCS 320.
