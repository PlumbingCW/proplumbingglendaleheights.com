When a disposal problem is an emergency

A disposal that hums but will not turn is usually jammed, and it is safe to leave until a plumber arrives. A disposal that leaks into the cabinet, drains slowly into the dishwasher line or smells strongly of sewage needs prompt attention. Water under the sink damages cabinet floors quickly. Safety first.

Never put a hand or a tool into a disposal that is plugged in or wired in. Turn off the switch and unplug it or use the breaker before any work. Then call and tell us what the unit is doing.

Disposal or drain

Many sink problems blamed on the disposal are really drain clogs. If water backs up on the other side of a double sink or the dishwasher drain fills the sink, the clog may be in the trap or the kitchen branch. Our drain cleaning service handles that part.

Tell us what you see, and we decide on the phone which tool to bring. Grease is the common cause. It cools in the pipe and holds food waste, and it forms a plug further down the line.

Running cold water and avoiding grease and fibrous waste, such as celery and corn husks, protects both the disposal and the drain.

Installing a new disposal

We install replacements and first-time units. Motor power, feed type and noise insulation are the main choices. A continuous feed unit suits most kitchens, and a batch feed unit adds a safety cover. We connect the sink flange, the drain, the dishwasher line and the electrical.

If your sink is a double bowl, the drain layout matters, and we may add a trap fitting or adjust the connection. A second sink drain with an air gap or high loop on the dishwasher line prevents backflow. We check each of these as we install.

What affects the cost of disposal repair or installation

  • Repair or replace. A reset or unjam is a small job. A new unit adds the cost of the disposal.
  • Unit size. More powerful models cost more.
  • Electrical. A switch or outlet that needs changing adds work.
  • Plumbing changes. Double-sink drains or old traps may need replacing.

A worn-out disposal is not covered by homeowner's insurance. A sudden leak that damages a cabinet or floor may be, so photograph it and check with your insurer.

What to keep out of the disposal

Do not put grease, bones, coffee grounds in large amounts, fibrous vegetable scraps, pasta or rice into a disposal. They bind in the trap and cause the clogs we see most. Run cold water before and after you use the unit. A few ice cubes help clean the blades, though they do not sharpen them.

Safety steps for a jammed or leaking disposal

  • Switch the disposal off at the wall and unplug it or flip the breaker before any work
  • Never put a hand or a utensil into the grinding chamber
  • Do not use chemical drain cleaner in a sink with standing water
  • Place a bucket under any leak and dry the cabinet floor
  • Keep children and pets away until the plumber arrives
