Addison mixes homes and industry

Addison grew from a settlement along Army Trail Road, which Winfield Scott built on top of a Potawatomi trail in 1832, into a village of about 35,700 people. It began to suburbanize in the 1960s on former farmland, and it also holds a large industrial district. Logistics, manufacturing and food distribution are all part of its economy.

That means two kinds of calls. Homeowners ask about drains and heaters. Businesses ask about floor drains, restroom plumbing and after-hours leaks. Our commercial plumbing team handles the second kind at any hour.

Industrial and food-service drains

A warehouse floor drain that backs up can halt work. Food service tenants fight grease constantly, and a line that clogs on a Friday evening needs an answer that night. We scope the line with a camera and, for buildup, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall.

Shared lines between tenants make blame hard to assign, so we record what we find. Footage and photos help landlords and tenants sort responsibility.

Homes built from the 1960s

The housing here is younger than in Glen Ellyn and older than newer subdivisions farther west. Homes from the 1960s often have their first or second water heater and their original drain runs. We look at what you have and give you a clear price. Addison sits on Salt Creek, a tributary of the Des Plaines River.

Heavy rain raises water tables across the area, so a sump pump that stops working in a storm is an emergency. Sump pump repair and installation is one of our common calls in storm season.

What an Addison call looks like in practice

A typical weekday call from a home is a slow main drain or a heater that stopped. A typical evening call from a business is a floor drain that will not clear before the next shift. A weekend call is often a washing machine hose or a toilet supply line that let go while the family was out.

Tell the dispatcher your street and the nearest cross street, such as Army Trail Road, Lake Street or Addison Road. Ask whether the water is clean or from a drain, because that decides whether the plumber brings a camera or a repair kit. We would rather ask three questions than send the wrong truck.

Water supply

The Village of Addison is a contract customer of the DuPage Water Commission. The Commission describes its Lake Michigan water as about 8 grains per gallon, a moderate hardness. Itasca and Wood Dale, to the north, are also contract customers and are covered through this page. Call and ask whether a plumber can reach your address.
