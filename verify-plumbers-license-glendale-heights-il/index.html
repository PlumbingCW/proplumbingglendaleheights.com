How to Verify a Plumber's License in Glendale Heights, IL

Before a plumber starts work in Glendale Heights, check three things: the state license, the plumbing contractor registration and the Village bond. The Illinois Department of Public Health licenses plumbers, and it hosts an online tool for checking a license. The Village requires plumbers working for hire on water heater installations to be registered and bonded.

This guide explains each check and what to do if a plumber cannot show proof.

By the Glendale Heights Pro Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-16 · Updated 2026-11-16

☎ (630) 822-9290

Why checking matters

Plumbing is a regulated trade because mistakes can hurt people. Bad gas connections, poor venting, cross-connected water lines and hidden leaks are real risks. A license shows that a plumber has met the state's requirements, and registration and insurance show that a contractor can cover mistakes.

Checking takes a few minutes, and a plumber who is proud of their credentials will not mind. Be cautious about anyone who gets defensive or offers a very low price for cash with no paperwork.

Ask for the license number and the contractor registration. Ask for a certificate of insurance and, for water heater work, proof of registration and bond with the Village. A legitimate plumber supplies these without fuss.

How the state system works

Under the Illinois Plumbing License Law, the Department of Public Health licenses plumbers, plumbing contractors and apprentice plumbers. A licensed plumber who does work for another person is a plumbing contractor. The state requires such a contractor to register and pay an annual fee, and to carry the insurance coverages the law lists.

The state's online tool lets you search a license. If a name does not appear, ask for the exact name on the license, because plumbers sometimes work under a company name that differs from the licensee. If you still cannot find the license, treat that as a red flag.

Glendale Heights and Illinois sources to rely on

The Illinois Department of Public Health's plumbing page describes the licensing law, contractor registration and the insurance a contractor must carry, and it links to the license verification tool. It also lists the Illinois Plumbing Code under Title 77 of the Illinois Administrative Code.

The Village's water heater brochure says every water heater installation needs a permit, that contracted installations require a licensed plumber who is registered and bonded with the Village, and that homeowners may install a heater in their own home with a permit.

Its sump pump brochure says contractors must be licensed and bonded with the Village before sump pit or draintile permits are issued. Community Development at (630) 260-6030 can confirm what applies to your project.

Red flags to watch for

A plumber who asks you to pull the permit yourself for work you are paying them to do deserves a second look. So does one who will not give a written price, who demands full payment up front or who uses a personal name with no company or license.

Unmarked trucks are not always a problem, but a refusal to show a license is. Beware of a price that is far below every other quote. It may omit permits, parts or proper venting.

What a good quote includes

A written quote lists the work, the parts, the permit, the haul-away and the price. It names the company and the license number. It states how long the work takes and what the warranty covers. It is dated and signed, and it can be compared with other quotes line by line.

We give you a clear price before work begins. If the scope changes, we stop and talk. That is our standard, and you should expect it from any plumber.

Special cases

For gas work, such as a tankless or a gas range hookup, the stakes are higher, and venting and gas sizing must be correct. See tankless water heater for why. For work on the sewer lateral or a service line, ask whether the Village's Public Works must inspect, and see sewer line repair.

Compare before you decide

Call at least two plumbers for any large job. Ask each for the same proofs and compare. The right choice is rarely the cheapest or the most expensive. It is the plumber who explains the work clearly and shows proof. Our about page explains how we work, and the plumbing installation page lists planned jobs.
