Tankless Water Heater No Water: Glendale Heights, IL Guide

When a tankless water heater gives no hot water, check four things in order: power to the unit, the gas supply, the water flow and any error code on the display. Most no-hot-water complaints trace to one of those, and several can be fixed in minutes. If the unit still will not fire, a plumber should diagnose it, because gas and venting faults are not a do-it-yourself job.

This guide walks through each check and explains what the answers mean.

By the Glendale Heights Pro Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-16 · Updated 2026-11-16

Step one: power

A tankless heater needs electricity even when it burns gas. Check that the unit is plugged in and that the outlet works. Reset the GFCI or breaker. Look at the display. A blank screen means no power. A lit screen with an error code means the unit is powered and has found a fault.

If the unit lost power and came back on, it may need a minute to restart. Run a hot tap and wait before you assume it has failed.

Step two: gas

Check that the gas valve to the unit is open. A gas valve handle in line with the pipe is open. If other gas appliances, such as the range, also fail, the gas supply to the house may be interrupted, and the gas utility should be called.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first. Do not operate switches.

Step three: flow

Tankless units fire only when the water flowing through them exceeds a minimum rate. If flow is too low, the burner will not start. A clogged inlet screen, a partly closed isolation valve or scale in a faucet can all drop flow below that threshold. Open a hot tap fully.

If flow is weak at every fixture, check the isolation valves and the inlet filter. If only one fixture is weak, the aerator or shower head may be scaled. Our guide to cleaning the inlet screen is a good starting point for the flow check.

Step four: the error code

Codes differ by brand, and the manual lists them. Typical categories are ignition failure, flame loss, overheating, vent blockage, flow sensor failure and condensate drain trouble. Record the code and the model number and have them ready when you call. Do not keep resetting a unit that throws the same code repeatedly.

A persistent code means a part or a condition needs attention. Repeated attempts to ignite can also flood the burner area with unburned gas on some faults.

Cold weather and condensate

A high-efficiency tankless unit produces acidic condensate that drains through a small line. In cold weather, an exposed condensate line can freeze and block, and the unit then shuts down on a drain fault. Check for a frozen line if the problem started on a very cold night.

A unit in an unheated garage or a crawlspace is more exposed. A plumber can insulate or reroute the drain.

Water facts that matter for a Glendale Heights tankless unit

The DuPage Water Commission describes the Lake Michigan water it supplies as about 8 grains per gallon, or 137 mg/L as calcium carbonate, and says it contains chlorine. The Village of Glendale Heights buys that water and states in its 2024 water quality report that it does not add chemicals to it.

Moderate hardness means scale builds inside a tankless heat exchanger over time, and scale is one of the most common reasons a unit loses flow and then stops firing. For questions about your own water, the Village report points residents to Public Works at (630) 260-6040.

Any water heater installation in the Village needs a permit, according to the Village's water heater brochure, so a replacement unit is not a same-day swap without that step.

When it is scale

If the screen is clean and flow is still low, and if the unit has not been flushed in a year or more, scale is likely. A descaling flush with a pump and a mild acid solution restores flow. It should be done once a year in moderately hard water.

A water softener reduces the scale load for every fixture.

When to call a plumber

Call a plumber if the unit shows a gas, flame or vent code, if you smell exhaust, if the cleaning steps do not restore hot water or if the unit leaks. Do not remove gas fittings or vent pipes yourself. Our tankless service team reads codes, tests gas and venting and repairs or replaces the failed part.

If the unit is beyond repair, water heater replacement covers your options, including a return to a tank heater.
